After the v3 benchmark: what we found, fixed and plan

14–15 October 2026 · from v3's first benchmark result to the v4 plan · the team ·

1. Where we started (14 Oct evening)

v3 seed 42 trained in ~2 hours on one RTX 4090. On the public sets: benchmark F1 89.5 (Run B 88.9); 3 of 7 public release rules passed (B1, C1, C2); B2, C3, C4 and C13 failed. Not releasable, and only slightly better than Run B on the benchmark, so we stopped to find out why, assuming the data might be wrong.

2. What the deep analysis found (ranked by impact)

#Root causeWhereEffect
1Wrong tokenizer shipped with every model. Training data was split into tokens with an extra punctuation step; the saved model didn't have itpipeline bug−4 benchmark points; B2 failed because of it
2Long values cut when windows are stitched. Full PEM keys (longer than one 512-token window) lost all but one piece; the model itself labelled them fullydecoder bugC13 failed; ~44% of each key left unmasked
3Contexts missing from training. 80% of the remaining errors are situations the data never shows with the right answer (a 3-digit number after "VÖEN", a company name after "surname", a card mid-sentence after "in the payment profile")data gap~620 false alarms, ~145 misses
4Look-alikes the model can't learn. Card/IBAN numbers that differ from real ones only by a hidden checksum: in prose, 45% of 16-digit numbers in our data were "not a card"data design~100 real cards missed; prose cards 17% found
5Wrong labels, mostly in the old corpus (half the data): ≈ 40 wrong labels per 1,000 (LLM ≈ 14, targeted 0). Unlabelled chat/signature names (~6,400), times labelled inside timestamps (~1,360), half-labelled names (~1,310), and 9,789 old documents never cleaned by the rules we applied to the LLM batcheslabel noisethe model learned the noise (repeats 81–92% of it)
6Our own privacy rules the benchmark disagrees with (7-letter FINs, phones in FIN fields, dates in building fields)policy~230 false alarms; precision only
Method: v3's predictions on the benchmark and probes; three independent analyses (coverage of benchmark contexts in training; label noise from all 633 disagreements on our own test data; labelling-rule consistency per source); every main claim re-checked.

4. Decisions made (D-51, 15 Oct)

  • Cards and IBANs are masked whatever their checksum (privacy first); the unlearnable checksum look-alikes leave the data.
  • Our FIN and date privacy rules stay; the benchmark/shadow items that contradict them go to the policy bucket.
  • Chat and log line times ([10:16] Elvin:) are not TIME.
  • Rule C13: the rules' track joins pieces of one key, like our product decoder.
  • The v4 data plan: clean the existing data (D-51g) and add the missing contexts (D-51h).
